My opinion

  1. Yes, it is worth building, and it is different from progress. Progress (Learning, Practising, Achieved…) says how far I have studied. These marks say what this text needs. Kept apart, neither muddies the other.
  2. Use the names our scholars already use, with plain words beside them:
    • a word that needs the dictionary is Gharīb (غريب), the science of Gharīb al-Qur'ān;
    • a word with several meanings in the Qur'an is Wujūh (وجوه);
    • an āyah explained by other āyāt is Tafsīr al-Qur'ān bil-Qur'ān;
    • an āyah explained by Hadith is bis-Sunnah;
    • one where the scholars' views across the eras matter is Aqwāl al-Mufassirīn;
    • one that is not complete on its own but belongs to its passage is Siyāq (سياق), "context";
    • one that needs the reason it was revealed is Asbāb an-Nuzūl.
    Your wording becomes the plain line; the term is the short label. The umbrella name I suggest is "Keys to understanding" (Bangla "বোঝার চাবি").
  3. Two kinds of mark, kept apart.
    • "Hard for me" is personal. Each reader marks their own, like progress.
    • The other keys are facts about the text. Owner and Prime set them for the madrasah, like the Asma descriptions. Everyone sees them, and each reader can add their own on top.
  4. Let the computer suggest, never decide. 1,995 of the Qur'an's 4,832 dictionary words occur only once (مُّقْمَحُونَ here is one), so Gharīb can be suggested automatically, dashed and marked "to be checked", just as the dua words are. The passage for Siyāq can be suggested from the Ruku' (here 36:1–12).
  5. Every key should lead somewhere (the "what next" lines in the demo):
    • Gharīb opens Lane / Hans Wehr, already on the Word card;
    • Hadith opens the Hadith tab searched for this āyah;
    • other āyāt opens the Āyah card's "Connected āyāt";
    • Siyāq opens the passage as a Range;
    • scholars and Hadith findings are written in a Note, which the Notes pane already holds.
  6. It fits the Ten Steps file: these keys are the doorways to its later steps (Hadith, scholars, context). That file can later say which key belongs to which step.
