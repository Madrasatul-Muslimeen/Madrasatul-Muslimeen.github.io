## What was built

Two new entries in every Dua Card's menu (R2's D22 menu): **🖼 Poster** and **Copy / share**. Both are drawn from
what the open card shows (so the "Without vowels" switch is respected), by the new Dua-owned module
`app/js/dua-poster.js`.

- **D07 the A4 poster.** The card as one A4 page: the top banner frame (D12), the title (D05), the Arabic, and the
  bottom banner (D11). Switches: **Arabic**, **English**, **Bangla**, **Word by word**; it starts with Arabic and the
  reader's own language. The text (never the frame or the banners) is sized so the page is filled and holds everything:
  a short dua grows up to 1.8 times, a long one shrinks (the longest Hisn al-Muslim dhikr, 1864 letters, fits).
  **🖨 Print** prints only the poster, on one A4 page; the page around it is hidden for the print and put back after.
- **D14 copy and share.** "What to include": Arabic, English, Bangla, Word by word, Source. "Share it as": **Text**
  (a preview, **Copy**; when the browser refuses the clipboard the text is selected and the page says to copy it with
  the device's Copy; **Share ↗** where the device offers sharing) or **A poster picture** (the poster drawn as an A4
  PNG, 1240 × 1754; **Save picture**, and **Share ↗** where the device can share files).

## What there is to show, honestly

- **English and Bangla** are a dua's *own* meaning only. Today that is the **21 Prophets' duas** (their āyahs'
  translations). A Hadith-book dua's HadeethEnc text retells the whole hadith, so it is not offered as the dua's
  meaning; Hisn al-Muslim has no translation in the app. For those the two switches are off and the pop-up says why.
- **Word by word** uses the Qur'an's meaning of each word, English and Bangla: every word of the Prophets' duas (new
  in `groups.json`, from the Qur'an data), and, for a Hadith-book dua, every word the computer matched to a Qur'an word
  (the same links, and the same fingerprint guard, as the card's word panel); an unmatched word shows "·". Hisn
  al-Muslim's adhkār have no word matches yet, so it is off for them, with the reason shown.
- A question for the Owner follows from this (J104): where a dua's own English and Bangla meaning should come from.
