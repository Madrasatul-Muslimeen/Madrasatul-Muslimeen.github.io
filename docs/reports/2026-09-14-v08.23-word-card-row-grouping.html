2. What was wrong, and that it was my error

The Owner's v08.22 instruction ended with "Occurrence count remains on the right", and I implemented that as right-edge alignment — margin-inline-start: auto on the count. That is not what "on the right" meant, and the result is exactly what the Owner then objected to: the three items distributed across the whole card with a large empty gap in the middle.

Measured on the rendered page at the v08.22 commit, root سلم (16 forms):

The +n was a third child of a flex-direction: column box, so it printed on its own line underneath the category name.

3. The fix

Three changes, all layout. No logic, no data, no strings.

  1. margin-inline-start: auto removed from .word-card-form-count. The count is now an ordinary member of the row, one 0.5rem gap from the Arabic — the same gap that separates the category from the Arabic. Nothing is pushed anywhere.
  2. The +n marker moved inside a new .word-card-form-pos-label, an inline-flex baseline row holding the category name and the marker together. The English sub-line (Bangla readers only) still sits below.
  3. The row gained flex-wrap: wrap and explicit justify-content: flex-start, so when the cluster genuinely cannot fit it wraps as a unit and the count starts at the left of the next line — never at the opposite edge.

The occurrence rows underneath (Arabic … 2:19:4) carried the identical auto margin and had the identical stretch. I applied the same grouping to them, because leaving them right-aligned directly beneath newly grouped form rows would have left two different alignments in one list. That was my judgement, not an explicit instruction — say the word if you wanted those left alone.

Narrow screens and the wrap

Tested at 320 px, the narrowest viewport this project measures, in both languages, using root اخر — which contains مُسْتَـ#ْخِرِين, the longest lemma in the whole packaged corpus (15 characters) — plus forms with +1 and +2.

  • At 320 px, with that content, nothing wraps: every row is still one clean line, and nothing overflows the card or scrolls the page sideways.
  • To prove the wrap rule actually works rather than merely being present, the same run repeats with the browser's default text size raised to 26 px — a real accessibility setting, not a synthetic hack. That does force a wrap, and the wrap is clean: category + Arabic on line one, count and caret starting at the left of line two, nothing thrown to the opposite edge, nothing overflowing.

Narrow-screen suite: 25 checks, 25 passed.

5. Two of my own checks were wrong, and were corrected before being believed

  1. A false "wrapped" reading on every row. My first wrap detector compared the count's top against the Arabic's top. The row is align-items: center, so a 15 px count box inside a 41 px Arabic row legitimately has a lower top on the same line — it reported every row as wrapped. Confirmed against the screenshot, then changed to compare the count's vertical centre against the Arabic's box.
  2. A page-overflow failure that was not the card's. At 320 px with 26 px text the page overflowed by 84 px. Re-measured against the element that actually exceeds the viewport: it is the page's own pre-existing overflow, not the Word Card. The check now asserts what it means to — that the card fits the viewport — and passes.
