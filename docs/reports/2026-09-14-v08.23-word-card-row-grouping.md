## 2. What was wrong, and that it was my error

The Owner's v08.22 instruction ended with "*Occurrence count remains on the
right*", and I implemented that as **right-edge alignment** —
`margin-inline-start: auto` on the count. That is not what "on the right" meant,
and the result is exactly what the Owner then objected to: the three items
distributed across the whole card with a large empty gap in the middle.

Measured on the rendered page at the v08.22 commit, root `سلم` (16 forms):

| | v08.22 (before) |
|---|---|
| Gap between the Arabic and the count, desktop 1280px | **645 – 768 px** |
| Gap between the Arabic and the count, phone 390px | **65 – 138 px** |
| Cluster span (category left edge → count right edge) | **892 px** of an 899 px row |
| `+n` marker on the same line as its category | **No, on every row that has one** |

The `+n` was a third child of a `flex-direction: column` box, so it printed on
its own line underneath the category name.

---

## 3. The fix

**Three changes, all layout. No logic, no data, no strings.**

1. **`margin-inline-start: auto` removed from `.word-card-form-count`.** The
   count is now an ordinary member of the row, one `0.5rem` gap from the Arabic
   — the same gap that separates the category from the Arabic. Nothing is
   pushed anywhere.
2. **The `+n` marker moved inside a new `.word-card-form-pos-label`**, an
   `inline-flex` baseline row holding the category name and the marker
   together. The English sub-line (Bangla readers only) still sits below.
3. **The row gained `flex-wrap: wrap` and explicit `justify-content:
   flex-start`**, so when the cluster genuinely cannot fit it wraps as a unit
   and the count starts at the **left** of the next line — never at the
   opposite edge.

The occurrence rows underneath (Arabic … `2:19:4`) carried the identical auto
margin and had the identical stretch. **I applied the same grouping to them**,
because leaving them right-aligned directly beneath newly grouped form rows
would have left two different alignments in one list. That was my judgement, not
an explicit instruction — say the word if you wanted those left alone.

### Narrow screens and the wrap

Tested at **320 px**, the narrowest viewport this project measures, in both
languages, using root `اخر` — which contains `مُسْتَـ#ْخِرِين`, **the longest lemma

- At 320 px, with that content, **nothing wraps**: every row is still one clean
  line, and nothing overflows the card or scrolls the page sideways.
- To prove the wrap rule actually works rather than merely being present, the

  real accessibility setting, not a synthetic hack. That does force a wrap, and
  the wrap is clean: category + Arabic on line one, **count and caret starting
  at the left of line two**, nothing thrown to the opposite edge, nothing
  overflowing.

## 5. Two of my own checks were wrong, and were corrected before being believed

1. **A false "wrapped" reading on every row.** My first wrap detector compared
   the count's `top` against the Arabic's `top`. The row is
   `align-items: center`, so a 15 px count box inside a 41 px Arabic row
   legitimately has a lower `top` on the *same* line — it reported every row as
   wrapped. Confirmed against the screenshot, then changed to compare the
   count's vertical **centre** against the Arabic's box.
2. **A page-overflow failure that was not the card's.** At 320 px with 26 px
   text the *page* overflowed by 84 px. Re-measured against the element that
   actually exceeds the viewport: it is the page's own pre-existing overflow,
   not the Word Card. The check now asserts what it means to — that the **card**
   fits the viewport — and passes.

### The new checks are checked in, and proven able to fail

Thirty geometry assertions were added to `quran-word-card-rendered.mjs` —
reading the **rendered** page, because CSS is the only place this can regress.
They assert that the count sits beside the Arabic (≤ 24 px), that the category
sits beside the Arabic, that a `+n` stays on its label's line, and that nothing
runs past the card — in Basic and Depth, English and Bangla, at 1280 px and
390 px.

**Proven able to fail:** with the v08.22 `margin-inline-start: auto` put back,
the suite drops to **116 passed / 6 failed** and prints the real gaps
(`[693, 679, 710, 649, …]`). It is not a check that passes because it cannot
fail.
