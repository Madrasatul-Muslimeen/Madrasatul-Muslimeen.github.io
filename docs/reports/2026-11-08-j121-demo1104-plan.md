# J121 demo1104 plan: auto-listen helper for a YouTube recitation (option 3), 8 Nov 2026

Owner, 4:02 pm 8 Nov: "J121: Let's try option 3. The reciter has got noticeable pause in between Ayah, we can try one Surah at a time." (PMS n121e = Q.)
This is the PLAN for a DEMO. Nothing is built. Demo first, then the Owner's approval, then build.

## What the Owner wants, in plain words
Press one button on a sūrah's YouTube video; the app listens to the pauses between āyāt by itself (no tapping), finds where each āyah starts and ends, checks the result, and keeps the times so the app can later play one āyah at a time from that video. One sūrah per video.

## The hard fact, stated plainly
A web page CANNOT read the audio of a YouTube video inside its player (the player lives on another origin). YouTube's official player API tells us only the playing time and state, never the sound. So "hearing the pauses" needs one of these honest routes:

| Route | What the Owner does | Works on | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Tab-audio share** (recommended for the demo) | Press "Listen for pauses", then in the browser's own box choose "This tab" and tick "Share tab audio" | Desktop Chrome and Edge only. NOT Firefox, Safari, iPhone, iPad, or Android (no tab audio there) | The only way to hear the sound with no tapping. The Owner presses once per sūrah, on a computer. Phones play the saved times afterwards; they never need to listen. |
| B. Tap-along | Taps at each āyah's start while the video plays | Every device | The tapping the Owner wants to avoid; kept only as a fallback and as a way to check route A. |
| C. Download the audio and analyse it | n/a | n/a | **Not allowed**: no copying of the audio (the channel's terms). Not offered. |

So: route A is found ONCE on a desktop, the times are saved, and every other reader on any device only plays them.

## How route A works (what the demo proves)
1. The page embeds the video with YouTube's own player (iframe API). No audio file is fetched, stored or copied.
2. The Owner presses "Listen for pauses" and shares the tab with its sound (`getDisplayMedia`, audio on; the video part of the share is discarded at once).
3. The sound is only measured in memory (loudness every ~20 ms, a Web Audio analyser). Nothing is recorded or saved except the times.
4. A pause is a stretch quieter than a threshold for at least a minimum length (the Owner said the pauses are noticeable; the demo shows the two numbers and lets the Owner move them with a slider, with the pauses drawn over a loudness line).
5. Each loudness frame is tied to the player's current time (`getCurrentTime()`, read every frame; the median offset removes the player's small delay). The pause's MIDDLE becomes the boundary between two āyāt, so a few hundred milliseconds of timing error cannot cut a word: the boundary sits in the silence.
6. The sūrah's boundaries are laid onto its āyāt in order: n āyāt need n-1 pauses.

## How the found times are checked (nothing is trusted until it passes)
- **Count check:** found pauses must equal āyāt - 1. If not, the demo lists the surplus or missing places (a long breath inside an āyah, or a short āyah with no clear pause) and offers: merge, split, or move a boundary by hand.
- **Length sanity check:** each āyah's length is compared with its Arabic word count; an āyah far shorter or longer than the sūrah's pace is flagged.
- **Listen check:** every āyah has a "play just this āyah" button (the saved start to end, through the same player), so the Owner hears each one; a tick per āyah turns it from "found" to "checked". An unchecked sūrah is shown as unchecked, never as correct (`checked:false`, as `era-speakers.json` does).
- **Machine check (for the build, not the Owner):** the pause finder is a pure function with a synthetic audio fixture (tones and silences of known length, with a noise floor and a breath inside an āyah), a mutation for each rule (threshold, minimum length, middle-of-pause, count check), run in `tools/i18n-verify` like every other suite.

## How they are saved (same shape as the Bangla reciter)
The Bangla reciter's times are `{ "<surah>": [ { verse_key, timestamp_from, timestamp_to }, ... ] }` in milliseconds (`gtaf_bangla_timestamps.json`). The YouTube times use exactly that list, one entry per āyah, in a new file beside it, with the video named:
`{ source: "youtube", videoId, surah, checked: false|true, ayahs: [ { verse_key, timestamp_from, timestamp_to } ] }`.
- Demo: the found times show on the page and can be **downloaded as a file** the Owner sends back; nothing writes to Firestore (a Firestore write needs Rules: an Owner Control Gate).
- Build (later, after approval): the Architect commits the checked file; the app reads it on first use only (I9). Playing one āyah = `seekTo(from)`, then stop when `getCurrentTime()` passes `to` (the boundary is inside a pause, so the poll's ±200 ms is harmless).
