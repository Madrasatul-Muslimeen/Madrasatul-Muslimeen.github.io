3. Data semantics — the A–E report

Everything below is measured across the whole packaged corpus (114 surah files, 77,429 words), not inferred. A checked-in test (the segment list matches the packaged corpus exactly) re-derives the segment set from the corpus on every run, so these numbers cannot drift silently.

A. What uniquely identifies each written form

morphology.lemma — the dictionary/citation form. 4,832 distinct values across 74,122 lemma-bearing occurrences (3,307 words carry no lemma).

This is the source's own identity key, not a convenience: where two different words share a spelling the corpus disambiguates them in the lemma string itself with a numeric suffix — مَٰلِك (Noun) and مَٰلِك2 (Proper Noun) are two entries under root ملك. Forms are therefore grouped by lemma and stay grouped by lemma.

B. What field supplies the grammatical / POS category

morphology.pos, and nothing else. Every word has one (0 missing).

It is not a single label. It is a " + "-joined chain describing the whole written token: attached proclitics, then the head word, then any attached pronoun — e.g. Determiner + Noun, Conjunction + Verb + Pronoun.

  • 359 distinct pos strings
  • built from exactly 46 distinct segments

The category of the word itself is the head: the last segment that is not Pronoun. That rule is implemented once, in the index builder, and is stated in the packaged file itself ("headRule").

C. Can one written form have multiple POS classifications?

Yes, but rarely — and v08.21 got the size of this wrong.

MeasureCountShare
Lemmas with more than one raw pos string2,067 of 4,83242.8%
Lemmas with more than one head category416 of 4,8328.6%
Lemmas with exactly one head category4,41691.4%

Categories per form: 1 → 4,416 · 2 → 391 · 3 → 18 · 4 → 3 · 5 → 3 · 7 → 1. Of the 416 ambiguous forms, 227 have a category holding ≥80% of their occurrences.

This is why v08.21's conclusion was wrong. v08.21 read the 2,067 figure — the raw-string count — and concluded that pos classifies a written token rather than a dictionary form, so no category could be shown. The 2,067 is almost entirely attached particles varying, not the word's own class changing. Once the clitics are accounted for, nine forms in ten have exactly one category. Genuine ambiguity does exist and is real linguistic ambiguity, e.g. رَّحِيم {Adjective 112, Noun 4}, يَوْم {Noun 324, Time Adverb 81}, ٱلَّذِى {Relative Pronoun 1442, Conditional 22}.

How the UI handles it: the dominant category is shown, a +n marker is placed beside it, and its title lists the others. A note under the list says how many forms in that list carry more than one. Nothing is flattened and nothing is hidden.

D. Can multiple distinct written forms share the same POS?

Yes, and it is the normal case. 2,881 distinct lemmas are Nouns; 1,461 are Verbs. Within a single root it is routine — root حمد has five forms:

FormCategory
حَمْدNoun
حَٰمِدُونNoun
مَّحْمُودNoun
حَمِيدAdjective
يُحْمَدُVerb

Three Nouns, and they must remain three separate rows. A checked-in test (two distinct written forms sharing a category stay two rows) guards exactly this, using a fixture whose two forms are both Nouns on purpose.

E. How attached particles affect the classification

They are what makes the raw pos misleading, and they are the whole reason v08.21 concluded no category was available.

ٱلْحَمْدُ is recorded as Determiner + Noun — the ٱل is a separate segment. The same lemma elsewhere appears as Noun, Preposition + Noun, Conjunction + Noun, and so on. Reading the raw string, the form looks like it belongs to four different categories; it is one Noun wearing four different sets of clitics.

Stripping to the head resolves this, and the size of the effect is the 2,067 → 416 drop in section C.

5. Correction 2 — the meaning of "Form" corrected

Form 1, Form 2, Form 3 are gone from the user interface entirely, in both Basic and Depth, in both languages. The translation key "Form {n}" is removed from bn.js, and the label is removed from the renderer's defaults, so there is no path by which such a string can reach the screen.

Each row now names the actual grammatical category the data supplies for that written form — Noun, Verb, Adjective, Time Adverb, and so on.

Distinct forms sharing a category are not collapsed. Rows are keyed by lemma, not by category; three Nouns under one root render as three rows, each with its own Arabic and its own count.

The two v08.21 explanatory notes were verified against the data before being touched, not deleted on sight:

6. Correction 3 — Basic card row layout

Required: [FORM/POS NAME] [ARABIC WORD] ........ [N OCCURRENCES]

A real CSS defect was found and is the root cause. v08.21 gave this rule to the row's own label span as well as to the toggle:

``css .word-card-form-toggle, .word-card-form > .word-card-form-n { … width: 100%; … } ``

In the Basic tab the row has no toggle and the <li> itself is the flex row — so the label claimed the full width, and the Arabic and the count were pushed out to the far edge. That is the stretching, in CSS. The label is no longer a row container, and the Arabic went from flex: 1 1 auto (which made it absorb all spare width) to flex: 0 0 auto. Only the count is pushed right, by its own margin-inline-start: auto.

8. Correction 5 — the Word Card no longer overlays the āyah

The defect: v08.20 kept the card open at the destination and carried the way back inside the card's own header. So the card sat over the āyah the reader had asked to see.

The fix, and what it deliberately does not do: the card is now closed on the way out — but its state is not destroyed. Before navigating, a full origin record is taken:

`` { occurrenceId, ref, surah, ayah, stageView, unitType, level, expandedForm, scrollY } ``

closeWordCard() also preserves the state object itself (level and occurrence survive a close, by design since Phase 2). The card is hidden, the āyah is displayed, and nothing overlays it.

Verified by geometry, not by an element's absence: the acceptance run computes the card's bounding box against #ayahPanels and asserts the two rectangles do not intersect, and that computed display is none for both mounts — the standing rule about [hidden] being overruled by a class rule.

9. Correction 6 — the deliberate "← Back to Word Card" control

A new control, #wordCardReturnBar, sits on the study screen above the āyah and outside the card. It carries the same data-word-card-origin-back identity the v08.20 control had, so the existing handler and every existing assertion keep working — this is a relocation, not a replacement.

Pressing it restores, in one action:

  • the original word (its permanent occurrence id)
  • the study view it was read in (Read or Note)
  • the Study Unit type in force at the time
  • the Word Card level (WbW / Basic / Depth)
  • the expanded form with its occurrence list populated
  • the scroll position

The expanded form is re-opened inside hydrateWordCardOccurrences(), where the forms index actually lands — asking earlier leaves a row reading aria-expanded="true" with nothing under it, which is a real defect this project has already shipped once.

10. Correction 7 — navigation safety

The control appears only when the āyah was reached from the Word Card. An origin record is written in exactly one place, goToWordOccurrence(). Ordinary Study or Note navigation never writes one, and the bar renders nothing without one.

Asserted on the live page before any word is ever opened: the bar exists, is hidden, and computes to display: none.

The origin is cleared — and the bar re-rendered away — when the reader opens any word directly, or closes the card. Only the first hop records an origin, so a chain of jumps always returns to the word the reader actually started from.

Card → Āyah A → Back → Āyah B → Back was run end to end at three viewport/language combinations. The second hop closes the card and offers Back again; the second Back returns to the same original word.

13. Flagged, not changed

A pre-existing data-notation issue in the packaged lemma text. 262 of the 4,832 lemmas (5.4%) carry a ^, a # or a digit inside the Arabic — e.g. ا^بَاء, سَمَا^ء, ـ#َٰن, مَٰلِك2. Verified against the written text: ا^ corresponds to ءَا (the madda-alef sequence) at every occurrence checked, and the digit is a homograph disambiguator.

On screen this reads as corrupted Arabic. It is not this tranche's doing — it has been visible in the Basic card's Lemma line since Phase 2 and in the form list since v08.21 — and it is deliberately not changed here, because correcting it means transforming the packaged corpus's own text, which is a data decision for the Owner, not a layout correction. Raised for a decision; the fix, if wanted, is a normalisation step in the data-pull tool with its own verification.

The eleven opaque POS abbreviations (section 3) are shown verbatim. If their expansion can be supplied from an authoritative source, they become ordinary translatable categories with no code change beyond the catalogue.

One new on-demand fetch. lemma-pos-index.json (159KB) is fetched the first time a root's derived forms are shown, alongside the two indexes that path already loads, and cached thereafter. Nothing joined the startup path (I9); the load-speed contract is untouched.

15. What the Owner should check, in the live app

Five minutes. Open the app, confirm the badge beside the name reads v08.22.

  1. Open a word. Study → Read, turn word-by-word on, tap any Arabic word.
  2. Basic Arabic tab. Under the derived forms you should see rows reading category · Arabic word · … · N occurrences — e.g. Noun ٱسْم … 381 occurrences. No row should say "Form 1". The category and the Arabic should sit next to each other on the left; the count alone on the right.
  3. Switch the app to Bangla and look again: the category should read বিশেষ্য, with the English underneath. A few rare particles will still show short English codes like RES — that is deliberate and explained above.
  4. Arabic in Depth tab. Same rows. Tap one to expand it, then tap an occurrence. The card should disappear and you should be looking at that āyah with nothing on top of it, with a ← Back to Word Card button above it.
  5. Press Back. You should land on the word you started from, on the Depth tab, with the same form still expanded and its occurrences listed. Do it a second time with a different occurrence — it should behave identically.
  6. Check the opposite: move around with the ordinary Study controls. The "Back to Word Card" button should never appear.

Known issue that may affect your test: some lemmas display a ^ or a digit inside the Arabic (e.g. سَمَا^ء). That is the packaged data's own notation, is not new in this version, and is flagged in section 13 for your decision.
